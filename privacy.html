7. Third-Party Services, Analytics, and Advertising

We use the following third-party services to run our business, serve this website, and measure our advertising. The first group handles information you give us on purpose. The second group collects information about your visit automatically.

Services that handle information you give us:

  • LeadConnector (via GoHighLevel): Customer relationship management and SMS messaging delivery. This is where your inquiry and your contact details are stored.
  • Google Workspace: Email, calendar scheduling, and file storage. This holds our correspondence with you and your booking details.
  • Vimeo: Video hosting and playback for films delivered to our couples.

Services that collect information about your visit automatically:

  • Google Analytics 4 and Google Tag Manager: Website analytics. These record which pages you visit, how long you stay, how you found us, your approximate location from your IP address, and your device and browser. Google Tag Manager is the container that loads our other tags.
  • Microsoft Clarity: Website analytics and session recording. This one records how you move through and interact with the page, and it is described on its own in Section 8.
  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram): Advertising, advertising measurement, and direct messaging. Two separate things happen here, and both are worth stating plainly. First, the Meta Pixel runs in your browser on our pages and reports page views and actions such as submitting an inquiry form. Second, when an inquiry comes in, our own server separately sends Meta a matching event through the Conversions API, which can include a hashed (scrambled) form of the email address and phone number you gave us, so that Meta can connect the inquiry back to an ad you clicked. Meta also processes your messages and delivers our replies when you contact us on Instagram or Facebook Messenger.
  • Cloudflare: Website hosting, security, and basic traffic analytics. As the service that delivers this page to you, Cloudflare sees your IP address and request details.

The providers in the first group process your information to support the delivery of our services, under their own privacy policies and terms.

The second group is different, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Cloudflare receive information about your visit directly from your browser. That is simply how website analytics and advertising measurement work. Each of them operates under its own terms and its own privacy policy, and those terms allow them to use data collected this way for their own purposes as well as ours, including improving their products and their advertising systems. We do not control that, and we are not in a position to promise you otherwise.

What we do not do is give any of them a list of our couples, sell your contact information, or share it with unrelated businesses so they can market to you.

You can read how these companies handle data in their own policies: Google Privacy Policy, Microsoft Privacy Statement, Meta Privacy Policy, and Cloudflare Privacy Policy.

8. Session Recording

Our website uses Microsoft Clarity, an analytics tool from Microsoft that does more than count visits. Clarity records how visitors move through and interact with our pages: mouse movement, scrolling, taps, clicks, and which parts of a page hold attention. Those recordings can be replayed afterwards, like a video of the session, and they are combined into heatmaps that show where people click and how far down a page they read.

We use this for one reason: to find the places where the website confuses people, so we can fix them. We are a husband and wife team, not a marketing department, and these recordings are reviewed occasionally, not monitored.

Microsoft Clarity masks the text typed into form fields by default, so what you type into our inquiry form is not meant to appear in the replay. Even so, please treat anything you type on this site as something that could be recorded, and do not enter sensitive information you would not want stored. The recordings are hosted and processed by Microsoft, not by us, under Microsoft’s own privacy terms.

You can read more in the Microsoft Privacy Statement. If you would rather not be recorded, you can block cookies from clarity.ms in your browser, or use a browser or extension that blocks analytics scripts. The site works normally either way.

9. Cookies and Similar Technologies

A cookie is a small file that a website stores in your browser. A first visit to flyiniris.com sets about thirteen of them, some by us and most by the analytics and advertising services named in Section 7. We do not currently show a cookie consent banner, so they are set when the page loads. They fall into three groups.

  • Analytics: Google Analytics cookies (their names begin with _ga) tell us whether you are a new or returning visitor and which pages you viewed. They last up to about 13 months.
  • Advertising measurement: a Google Ads cookie (_gcl_au) plus Microsoft and Bing cookies (MUID, MR, SRM_B, SM, ANONCHK) connect a later inquiry back to an ad or a search, so we can tell which advertising is actually worth paying for. These last from the length of a single browsing session up to about 13 months.
  • Session replay: Microsoft Clarity cookies (_clck, _clsk, CLID) tie the parts of one visit together into a single recording, as described in Section 8. These last from one day up to about a year.

None of the cookies listed above stores your name, email address, or phone number. We do not read them ourselves to identify you personally; what we see is the reports these services build from them.

How to control them. Every major browser lets you block or delete cookies. In Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox, open Settings and look for Privacy, then Cookies or Tracking. You can block third-party cookies entirely, delete the cookies already stored for flyiniris.com, or browse in a private or incognito window, which discards them when you close it. Blocking them will not stop you using this website: nothing here depends on them.

You can also opt out of Google Analytics on every website at once using the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on, and adjust Microsoft advertising preferences at the Microsoft ad settings page. Most browsers also send a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal; note that our site does not currently act on those signals.
